While you were asleep last night, two pieces of software signed a contract. Nobody clicked anything.

Somewhere right now, your company's procurement system is negotiating a price with another company's supplier system.
No one's watching. No one's clicking. The contract gets signed in under a second.

This used to be science fiction.
A modern AI-enabled ERP can detect a raw material shortage, query a supplier's AI agent, compare it against three other suppliers' agents, negotiate based on real-time market data, and execute the purchase order all before anyone walks into the office.

The thing nobody quite talks about: a whole layer of commerce is now happening at a speed humans cannot witness in real time. We see the receipts in the morning.

Which raises a strange question for anyone in operations, procurement, or finance:
If the negotiation happens machine-to-machine, what exactly is the human's job?

It's not haggling anymore. It's writing the rules of how the bots haggle. Setting the floor, the ceiling, the acceptable trade-offs. We're moving from negotiators to lawmakers quietly, without a memo.

The org chart hasn't caught up yet. The skill set definitely hasn't.
