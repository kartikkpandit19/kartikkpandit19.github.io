Noise-cancelling earbuds don't seal sound out. They cancel it with more sound.

A tiny microphone listens to the noise heading for your ear. The earbud instantly plays a mirror image of that wave, the exact opposite shape. The two meet and flatten each other out.

The hum of the plane is still hitting you. You just also get its perfect negative, and the pair cancel to something close to silence.

It has to react in a sliver of a second, faster than the noise reaches your eardrum, again and again the whole flight.

The quiet isn't the absence of sound. It's two sounds erasing each other.

There is something inherently poetic about how electrons pulsing through silicon can be translated into the fluid responsiveness of a modern device. We have reached a point where the hardware has become a "canvas" and the software is the "brush," and the friction between them has been polished away almost entirely.
