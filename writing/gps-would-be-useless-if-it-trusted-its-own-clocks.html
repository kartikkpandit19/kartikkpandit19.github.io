GPS would be useless if it trusted its own clocks.

The satellites overhead move fast and sit far from Earth's gravity, and both of those bend time. Their clocks tick about 38 millionths of a second ahead of ours every day.

Sounds like nothing. It isn't. Left uncorrected, your location would drift by roughly 10 kilometres a day. Your map would put you in the next city by dinner.

So the system corrects for it constantly, accounting for a quirk of relativity that Einstein worked out decades before satellites existed.

Your phone finds the coffee shop by quietly correcting for the shape of time.
