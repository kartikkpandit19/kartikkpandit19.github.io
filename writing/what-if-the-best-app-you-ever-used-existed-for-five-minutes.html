What if the best app you ever used existed for five minutes and then deleted itself?

This is starting to actually happen.
A new class of AI systems can, on request, generate a tiny custom application for a single specific task : calculate a strange multi-currency invoice split, parse a peculiar one-off data export, run a niche compliance check and then delete the entire codebase once the answer is delivered.
No subscription. No login. No "this might be useful again later." Pure disposable software.
For most of computing history, software was something you acquired. You bought it, installed it, learned it, kept it. The whole SaaS industry is built on the premise that customers will hold onto their tools and pay monthly forever to keep doing so.

Ephemeral software inverts the premise.
The tool is sized exactly to the problem and then thrown away. Same way you don't keep the math you scribbled on a napkin to split a dinner bill.
Which raises a slow, uncomfortable question for anyone building horizontal SaaS( @Zoho, @Salesforce, @Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central ):
How much of what we sell is genuinely a long-term tool and how much is a one-off calculation our customer hasn't realized they could just ask for and discard?
Some of the most defensible products of the last decade may turn out to be expensive napkins.
