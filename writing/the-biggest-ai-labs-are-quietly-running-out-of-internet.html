The biggest AI labs are quietly running out of internet. What they do next is more interesting than the problem itself.

The big AI labs have a problem they don't love talking about.
They're running out of internet.
Models have effectively read everything humans have written and put online. The next generation of training data has to come from somewhere and increasingly, that "somewhere" is other AI.
AI training on AI. Synthetic data.
Researchers have a name for what happens when this loop tightens too much: model collapse. Like photocopying a photocopy, each generation loses sharpness, weirdness, the rough edges of real thought. The reasoning gets vague. The model starts to forget what reality actually looks like.
What strikes me is the implication.
For most of the last decade, the consensus was that messy, biased, contradictory human writing was the bug and clean machine-generated text would be the upgrade.
It's looking like the opposite is true.
The messiness is the signal. The contradictions, the slang, the half-formed arguments, the wrong opinions confidently stated that's the texture of real cognition. Strip it away and the model starts dreaming of itself.
One of the most valuable resource in AI right now might be the one thing it can't manufacture: an actual human being, being themselves, online.
