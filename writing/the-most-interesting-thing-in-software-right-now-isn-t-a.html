The most interesting thing in software right now isn't a feature. It's the disappearance of features.

For thirty years, software has made the human bend.
We learn the menus. We memorize the shortcuts. We adapt our brains to its architecture.
Now something is quietly flipping.
A new wave of tools doesn't have a fixed interface at all. You tell it what you want... a chart, a comparison, a quick filter and it draws an interface just for that moment. When you're done, the screen dissolves back to blank.
No fixed dashboard. No "where's that menu again." The software reads your intent and builds a shape around it.
What strikes me isn't the engineering. It's the role reversal.
For decades, learning software was a job skill. We measured productivity by how well a human had memorized a tool. That entire muscle the one PMs, analysts, ops people built over careers is becoming obsolete on a different axis.

The software is finally the thing that has to adapt.
Which makes me wonder what else in our work life we've quietly trained ourselves to bend around not because it was right, but because the tool couldn't bend back.
