For 30 years, "using software" meant looking down. That bad posture is about to end.

Picture a warehouse manager who isn't looking at a tablet.
She's looking through a headset, and floating above every shelf is its real-time stock count. Heatmaps glow red over slow-moving SKUs. A green path traces itself along the floor to the next pick.
The ERP isn't on a screen. It's on the floor.
For thirty years, "interacting with software" has meant looking down at a phone, a laptop, a monitor. The user had to mentally translate between a 2D abstraction and a 3D world. Every operations manager, warehouse worker, surgeon, mechanic has spent their entire career bridging that gap by hand.
Spatial computing collapses the translation.
The data isn't represented in a spreadsheet anymore. It's painted directly onto the thing it describes.
What's quietly profound: for the first time in computing history, we're not asking the human to come to the data. The data is finally coming to the human.
The implications for any industrial SaaS company are uncomfortable. The product surface you've polished for a decade: the screen, may turn out to be a temporary container.
The next interface isn't a screen. It's the room.
