Some of the most expensive software in the world doesn't have a screen.

No login page. No dashboard. No buttons. Just an API endpoint and a price.
This is "headless" architecture, and it's quietly become the dominant model for serious infrastructure. @Stripe runs your payments. @Twilio sends your texts. @Algolia powers your search. None of them have a "product" in the way general users think about products. They have an interface for machines.
The think the shift is bigger than it looks.
For most of SaaS history, the screen was the product for CFOs, COOs, CEOs. UI design, onboarding flow, dashboards these were the moat. Customers paid for an experience as much as a function.
Headless flipped that. The customer doesn't need your screen. They want your math. Your compliance. Your global rails. They'll build their own screen, thank you very much.
What this changes about product strategy is enormous, and maybe most teams haven't caught up.
If your product is a screen, you're competing on UX. If your product is an API, you're competing on reliability, latency, documentation, and developer trust. Completely different game. Mostly a different team.
The most powerful software in your stack is probably already invisible. You just don't think of it as software anymore.
