A single AI query uses roughly ten times the electricity of a regular Google search.

Multiply that by a billion users, and you suddenly understand why Microsoft, Amazon, and Google are no longer just renting power from the grid.
They're funding nuclear reactors.
Specifically, a new generation of Small Modular Reactors physical, fission-based power plants being built primarily to feed AI data centers. Microsoft has signed a deal to restart Three Mile Island. Amazon bought a data center campus next to a nuclear plant. Google has signed contracts for SMR capacity that doesn't exist yet.
The companies that sold us the word "cloud" are now in the atomic energy business.
What I find oddly grounding about this: for a decade, software people loved to talk about the cloud like it lived somewhere abstract and weightless.
"Serverless." "Edge." "Virtual." A vocabulary designed to make you forget the physical world.
Turns out the physical world was just waiting.
Every AI feature your team is shipping right now is a vote for more electricity, more cooling water, more concrete, more uranium.
Software is, finally and unmistakably, a physical industry.
The energy bill always finds you. It just took a while.
