Part of the internet's security rests on a wall of lava lamps.

Computers are bad at being truly random. Ask code for a random number and you get a formula pretending, predictable enough that a determined attacker can sometimes guess it.

So Cloudflare, which sits in front of a large slice of the web, points a camera at about 100 lava lamps in its lobby. The blobs drift and split in ways nobody can predict or repeat, and that footage becomes part of the raw chaos used to make encryption keys.

The unbreakable math under a lot of the internet leans, in part, on wax floating in water.

Some randomness you have to go out and find.
